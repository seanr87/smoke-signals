About Smoke Signals

Smoke Signals reads the public replays of King of the Sea XIX NA (171 games, 30 clans, 1547 smokes) and measures what every smoke screen did: where and when it was laid, who it hid, what was fired from or through it, and what happened at the caps meanwhile. Both teams' replays are read and merged; a team's own replay gives its roster and consumables.

Method in brief

  • Smokes: every smoke-screen object the primary replay shows, matched to the ship that activated it (a flag marks the few whose owner is inferred from position alone).
  • Damage credit: each damage event in the replay ledger is classified by weapon from its timing and the shots in flight (shell, secondary, fire, airstrike, torpedo, flood, unknown). Main-battery shells, air-support bombs, torpedoes and the fires shells and bombs start count as smoke damage when the shot was fired from inside a smoke, or from outside by an undetected shooter whose line to an enemy in detection range crossed the smoke; a torpedo or an airstrike is placed where and when the ship launched it, and its concealed or detected part follows the launcher's spotted state then. Secondary, flood and unknown damage never earn credit; when a ship fired such a weapon from a smoke, the smoke's page lists those hits as not counted. Clan and player tables also carry a guns-only total (shells and fires), the round 6 definition, for comparison.
  • Concealed vs detected: by the shooter's visibility flags at the firing moment; the detected part is split by reason (radar, hydro, smoke fire penalty, aircraft, proximity, surface lit).
  • Lenses: most boards default to destroyer smokes; "all" includes cruiser and battleship smokes. Tags (purpose, movement, coverage) are rule-based from the replay, with a confidence where the rule is fuzzy.
  • Minimum samples: clan boards and clan awards need 7 games; Best in Haze needs 10 smokes; Most Selfish Smoker 5; the leaderboard hides players under 5 smokes by default.
  • Awards: award names are tongue-in-cheek; they describe a single smoke, not a player.
  • Round 6: Round 6 (H-SR, RESIN, PN only) is archived at git tag site-r6-archive.

Data limits

  • Torpedo damage: the replay ledger classifies about 77% of the torpedo damage the game's own results report. A replay only carries hits near its recorder, and hits that fall under the classifier's floor are counted as unknown, not as shells, so they never inflate smoke damage. Torpedo totals on this site are what the two replays saw.
  • Airstrikes: about 44% of the Dutch ships' air-support bomb damage is classified as airstrike. The rest is counted as main-battery shells (when it lands with a salvo) or unknown; the share credited to smokes is small either way, since most strikes are called from outside smoke while spotted.
  • Ledger drift: a game whose merged ledger exceeds the game's own totals by more than 25% is flagged on its page and left out of awards and leaderboards (0 games in this build). Three games whose two replays' clocks drift within the game (PN vs HTDOG, BTLST vs MZY, MANBA vs KM) sit at 1.10 to 1.16, inside the normal tail, and carry a note on their pages.
  • One-sided games: KSC vs -BN- | Playoffs Losers Bracket LB Round 1 | Game 3 (one replay; -BN-, KSC smokes inferred, no damage counted).
  • Coverage: smokes come from the primary replay only; a smoke neither recorder saw is not in the table. Charges, rosters and consumables come from a team's own replay.
  • Owner attribution: 103 of 1547 smokes carry an owner flag (no activation within 5 s of the smoke's creation); their owner is the nearest ship with a smoke consumable.

Key terms

  • Smoke damage: main-gun shells, air-support bombs and torpedoes fired from or through a smoke by the smoking team, plus the fires shells and bombs started; each shot is credited to one smoke. Concealed damage is the part fired while the shooter was undetected; detected damage is the rest.
  • Concealed smoke damage: the part of the smoke damage fired while the shooter was undetected. Detected damage is the rest, broken down by how the shooter was spotted (radar, surface lit, hydro, proximity, aircraft, smoke fire penalty).
  • DD damage: smoke damage, concealed and detected, dealt to enemy destroyers; same credit rules as smoke damage.
  • Selfish smoke: no teammate other than the owner was ever inside it, and no teammate fired through it from behind. A tendency, not a flaw: some ships (Gdansk, for one) smoke selfishly by design. How often a clan or player smokes selfishly is a style signal.
  • Radar bait: a smoke where enemy radar spotted our ships inside it. Possibly a mistake: smokes are often laid knowing radar is a risk, and one spot is not a failure, but a high rate of radar-punished smokes for a clan or player is a warning sign.
  • Contested cap: A cap is contested for a team when it sits on the midline between the two spawns, lies on the enemy's side of the midline, or is held by the enemy.

Clan metrics

  • uptime: seconds per game with at least one of the clan's smokes standing.
  • first_smoke: seconds since battle start of the clan's first smoke in a game, averaged over the games with a smoke in the lens.
  • charge: smoke charges used over charges carried by the clan's smoke ships.
  • stacking: share of the clan's smokes that overlapped another smoke.
  • contested: share of smokes that stood within 1 km of a contested cap on average over their life.
  • swing: change in the point gap (ours minus theirs) from activation to capture, averaged.
  • deaths: ships of the clan that a smoke concealed (inside it, or firing from behind it), were then detected, and sank while the smoke stood or within 20 s after its last puff faded.
  • kills: enemy ships whose last damage before sinking was a shell, fire tick, bomb or torpedo credited to the smoke.
  • punished: share of smokes in which at least one of the clan's ships was radar-spotted while inside. Possibly a mistake: smokes are often laid knowing radar is a risk, and one spot is not a failure, but a high rate for a clan or player is a warning sign.
  • concealed: the part of the smoke damage fired while the shooter was undetected. Detected damage is the rest, broken down by how the shooter was spotted (radar, surface lit, hydro, proximity, aircraft, smoke fire penalty).
  • dd: smoke damage, concealed and detected, dealt to enemy destroyers; same credit rules as smoke damage.
  • guns: main-battery shells and the fires they start, the round 6 definition, without airstrikes and torpedoes.
  • torpedo: torpedoes launched from inside the smoke or from behind it by an undetected ship.
  • offensive: enemies spotted by the clan's own radar run from inside its smoke while concealed, inferred from the activation and radar range.
  • won: a cap within stock gun range of a concealed ship flipped to the clan during the smoke or within 10 s after it.
  • coverage: share of a contested cap's area within the range of a radar ship concealed in the smoke.
